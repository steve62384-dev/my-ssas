Business Owners missing out on Billions from government

By Steve Hunt · Written 18 October 2022

Archived opinion piece, written in October 2022. Its figures, examples and calls to action are historical, not current recommendations. Please do not use them to decide whether to set up a SSAS, move money into one, or invest through one.

Some rules and figures have changed since. See what has changed at the end of this post, and the SSAS pension FAQ for today’s rules.

To all you accountants and business owners out there, a possible financial resolution. Please correct me if I am wrong. Steve@My-SSAS.co.uk

An Ideal scenario for Salary Sacrifice (now termed Salary Exchange):

Mr Smith owns his business and also personally owns his business premises worth £86k. He is earning £200k p.a.

Through a SSAS he decides to give up £2,208.66 net of his £16,666.67 per month gross salary.

Based on current tax rates:

Old monthly gross payNew monthly gross pay
£16,666.67£12,500.00
Tax£5,774.95£3,952.37
EE NIC£821.79£686.37
Take Home Pay£10,069.92£7,861.26
ER NIC£2,394.20£1,767.12

Difference in take home pay £10,069.92 less £7,861.26: a net sacrifice of £2,208.66

Gross salary sacrificed = £16,666.67 less £12,500.00:

£4,166.67

So instead of the employer paying the £4,166.67 to the employee he pays into the employee’s pension instead. Thus, it will only cost the employee £2,208.66.

But…… the salary reduction will also save the employer on his company/employer NIC of £2,394.20 less £1,767.12 :

£627.08

The employer can then add this to the salary sacrifice amount which is a cost neutral position for the employer taking the pension payment to:

£4,166.67 plus £627.08 Totalling:

£4,793.75 at a cost to the employee of £2,208.66 with the extra £2,585.09 paid by HMRC.

On an annual basis this equates to:

£26,503.94 Sacrificed

£57,525.00 Paid into pension fund at zero cost to employer

Then, through the SSAS, for commercial property purchase, it is possible to borrow 50% of the value of the SSAS. As such the SSAS fund of £57,525.00 can be leveraged up to a total purchase value of:

£86,287.50

The SSAS can then buy the business premises from Mr Smith for £86,000 (ignoring any possible CGT here).

If Mr Smith were to use the £86k to make up additional salary sacrifices then over a couple of tax years he could also convert the £86k into a further £187,281.15. This could be placed into his pension fund using the same salary sacrifice principle.

So, over a couple of tax years by giving up just over £2k per month net salary he can have:

  1. Property in his SSAS worth £86,287.50 for which his business is also paying rent into his own pension (which he can take out tax free when he retires)
  2. Has an additional SSAS fund of £187,281.15 totalling a SSAS value of almost £274k
  3. This will have cost him £26,503.94

Note added in October 2026: rent paid to a SSAS becomes pension money. It cannot itself be taken out tax free. Usually up to 25% of pension benefits can be taken tax free, within the £268,275 lump sum allowance for most people, and the rest is normally taxed.

Certainly the £28k loan will have to be paid back but that could also come out of the rent payments.

What has changed since October 2022

  • National Insurance: in 2026/27 the employee main rate is 8% (2% above the upper limit) and the employer rate is 15%, so the 2022 figures in the table above no longer apply.

    Sources GOV.UK: Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027

  • Salary sacrifice: a 2026 Act gives the government power to charge National Insurance on salary or bonus given up for pension payments, above a yearly limit. The first limit must be £2,000. It applies from the 2029/30 tax year, which starts on 6 April 2029.

    Sources Law: National Insurance Contributions (Employer Pensions Contributions) Act 2026

  • Tax-free cash: money taken out of a pension is normally taxed as income. Usually up to 25% can be taken tax free, within an overall lump sum allowance of £268,275 for most people.

    Sources GOV.UK: Tax when you get a pension: what's tax-free · HMRC: PTM171000: lump sum allowance · This site: FAQ: How much can I take tax free?

  • Buying the premises: the SSAS must pay market value, and stamp duty land tax and other costs apply.

    Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles · GOV.UK: Stamp Duty Land Tax: non-residential and mixed rates · This site: SSAS commercial property

  • Where this 2022 post says HMRC “pays” or gives money, it means Income Tax and National Insurance that is not charged, plus any employer National Insurance saving the employer passes into the pension. HMRC does not pay anything into the SSAS itself.

    Sources MoneyHelper: Salary sacrifice and your pension
