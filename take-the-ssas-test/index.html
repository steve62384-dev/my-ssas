The SSAS Test

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Last checked 5 October 2026

Three quick questions about your business and your pensions. Each one says why we ask, with the rule behind it. Then add your details, and your answers go to Steve, who will get back to you.

The test does not tell you whether to set up a SSAS or move a pension. It is general information, not advice.

1. What kind of business do you have?

Why we ask: A SSAS is set up by an employer. For most people reading this, that is their own company. Any employer that sets up or joins the scheme is called a sponsoring employer.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.150(5) and (6) · HMRC: PTM022000: types of pension scheme

2. Has the business been trading for the last 12 months?

Why we ask: HMRC can refuse to register a new SSAS if a sponsoring company has been dormant. That means dormant for a whole month in a row, in the year before HMRC decides.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.153(5)(h) · HMRC: PTM032200: HMRC's decision

3. Roughly how much is in your pensions now?

Why we ask: SSAS fees are flat, so they weigh more on a smaller pension. A flat fee of £1,500 a year costs less than a 1% yearly charge once a pension is above £150,000, and more below it. Some providers also set their own minimum size.

Sources Provider evidence: SSAS provider fees compared, 2026 · Steve's analysis: The flat fee and 1% comparison
