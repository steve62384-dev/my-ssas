Autumn Statement 2022 ~ Salary Sacrifice/Salary Exchange better than ever

By Steve Hunt · Written 18 November 2022

Archived opinion piece, written in November 2022. Its figures, examples and calls to action are historical, not current recommendations. Please do not use them to decide whether to set up a SSAS, move money into one, or invest through one.

Some rules and figures have changed since. See what has changed at the end of this post, and the SSAS pension FAQ for today’s rules.

Not sure what is the best adjective, see-saw or yo-yo? Bit of a shambles isn’t it.

A few months back Salary Sacrifice was diminished with the abolition of 45% tax. Now we have the opposite. And every employer and employee in the country should be taking advantage.

Employers and employees are missing out on millions of pounds worth of payment from HMRC. Fact.

Large sums of money are available from HMRC using Salary Sacrifice / Salary Exchange. OK, it’s into your pension so you may not benefit immediately. But boy are you likely to need a good pension in the years to come.

First let me clear up the name.

This is a blog for SSAS pension, SSAS pensions & pension SSAS but the principle applies to everyone.

Salary Sacrifice is not a new concept. I was arranging salary sacrifice payments for FTSE100 directors 40 years ago. And it was the most senior of management who were benefitting from Salary Sacrifice.

But in the new ‘woke’ world we live in the word ‘sacrifice’ is too nasty. Poor darlings. So, the term is being ‘re-branded’ by the industry to Salary Exchange. I like Sacrifice.

What’s the big deal and how does it work

It is a huge deal and every employer in the UK should be offering Salary Sacrifice to all its employees. Why? Let me show you in numbers:

Anyone now earning over £125k p.a.

My previous blog goes into a great amount of detail on how it works:

Business Owners missing out on Billions from government

But, in summary. If you add both the employer and the employee NIC savings to the employees’ pension pot then:

£100 of take-home pay increases to £217.80 of pension contributions. That is a return of 117% on your money without having to invest a penny. And the best bit is that it’s HMRC that pay that extra £117.80 into your pension fund.

So, anyone now earning more than £125k p.a. can turn:

£100 of net pay into a fund of £217.80 With HMRC paying a whopping £117.80

Anyone earning over £50,279 p.a.(assuming 1257L tax code)

The numbers here are not that different:

£100 of take-home pay increases to £202.73 of pension contributions. That is a return of 102% on your money without having to invest a penny. And again, HMRC pay that extra £102.73 into your pension fund.

So, anyone now earning more than £50,279 p.a. can turn:

£100 of net pay into a fund of £202.73 With HMRC paying £102.73

Everyone else who receives more than the minimum wage

One of the few negatives of Salary Sacrifice is that employees can’t go below the minimum wage. Anyone on minimum wage cannot enjoy Salary Sacrifice. Anyone looking to take out a mortgage may also not benefit. But for everyone else who pays the basic amount of income tax the numbers are:

£100 of take-home pay increases to £172.36 of pension contributions. That is an immediate return of 72%. And again, HMRC pay that extra £72.36 into your pension fund.

So, anyone who is a basic rate taxpayer can turn:

£100 of net pay into a fund of £172.36 With HMRC paying £72.36

So why doesn’t everyone do this? Beats me. Apart from the few exceptions everyone should.

Summary

Regarding SSAS pension, SSAS pensions & pension SSAS as I explained in my Blog https://my-ssas.co.uk/business-owners-missing-out-on-billions-form-government/. Through a SSAS these funds can be leveraged or geared up further using commercial loan facility. For a higher rate taxpayer £100 of net pay can be leveraged up to a fund of £326.70. So, £1,000 of net pay can be leveraged to £3,267. Do that for a year and that’s almost £40k in your SSAS to help your business.

What has changed since November 2022

  • National Insurance: in 2026/27 the employee main rate is 8% (2% above the upper limit) and the employer rate is 15%, so the 2022 figures in this post no longer apply.

    Sources GOV.UK: Rates and thresholds for employers 2026 to 2027

  • Salary sacrifice: a 2026 Act gives the government power to charge National Insurance on salary or bonus given up for pension payments, above a yearly limit. The first limit must be £2,000. It applies from the 2029/30 tax year, which starts on 6 April 2029.

    Sources Law: National Insurance Contributions (Employer Pensions Contributions) Act 2026

  • Borrowing: a SSAS can borrow up to 50% of its net value just before the borrowing, worked out for each separate money purchase arrangement.

    Sources HMRC: PTM124000: borrowing · This site: SSAS commercial property: borrowing to buy

  • Where this 2022 post says HMRC “pays” or gives money, it means Income Tax and National Insurance that is not charged, plus any employer National Insurance saving the employer passes into the pension. HMRC does not pay anything into the SSAS itself.

    Sources MoneyHelper: Salary sacrifice and your pension
