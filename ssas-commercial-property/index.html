Can my SSAS buy my business premises?

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Last checked 5 October 2026

The short answer: yes. A SSAS can buy commercial property, including the building your company uses. Your company can then rent it at a full market rent, and the rent goes into the pension. The SSAS can buy from you or your company, but only at market value. It can borrow up to half of its net value to help. Homes are different: a SSAS that holds a home faces heavy tax charges.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles; PTM124000: borrowing · Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch 29A, paras 7, 8, 10 and 14

What a SSAS can buy

Commercial property

A SSAS can buy commercial property, such as offices, shops, factories, warehouses and land. That includes the building your company uses. Commercial property is not taxable property, so the heavy charges for homes do not apply.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles · Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch 29A, paras 7, 8, 10 and 14

Not homes

Homes are taxable property, wherever they are in the world. A SSAS that holds one faces heavy tax charges when it buys, every year it holds it, and on any gain when it sells. Some buildings do not count as homes, such as qualifying care homes, student halls, hospitals and a whole hotel.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.174A; Finance Act 2004, ss.185A to 185G; Finance Act 2004, Sch 29A, paras 7, 8, 10 and 14 · HMRC: PTM125200: residential property; PTM125300: direct holdings of taxable property

A flat above a shop

A flat counts as a home, even if it is part of a commercial building, unless an exception applies. One exception is a flat used with business premises the SSAS owns, such as a flat above a shop. The person living there must not be a member or connected with a member. Another is a flat or house for an employee who must live there as a condition of the job. That employee must not be a member, connected with a member, or connected with the employer.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch 29A, paras 7, 8, 10 and 14 · HMRC: PTM125200: residential property

Buying from you or your company

Market value only

The SSAS can buy from you, your company or anyone connected with you, but only at the price an unconnected buyer would pay. If it pays more than the property is worth, HMRC treats the extra as an unauthorised payment and taxes it. The same applies if it later sells to you or your company for less than the property is worth.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles; PTM133700: value shifting

Tax for the seller

If you sell a property at a gain, you may have Capital Gains Tax to pay. A limited company normally pays Corporation Tax on its gains instead.

Sources GOV.UK: Capital Gains Tax for business

Stamp duty land tax

The SSAS pays stamp duty land tax on the purchase in England and Northern Ireland. Scotland and Wales have their own versions. The return must be sent, and the tax paid, within 14 days of completion. A solicitor usually does this.

Sources GOV.UK: Stamp Duty Land Tax: non-residential and mixed rates; Stamp Duty Land Tax

VAT

Selling or letting commercial property is normally exempt from VAT. But if the owner has opted to tax the building, VAT is normally charged on the price and the rent. The freehold sale of a new commercial building, one completed less than three years ago, normally has VAT on it too. An option to tax does not pass to the buyer: the buyer decides whether to opt. Check the VAT position before the SSAS buys.

Sources HMRC: Land and property (VAT Notice 742); Opting to tax land and buildings (VAT Notice 742A)

Renting it to your company

Full market rent

Your company can rent the building from the SSAS, but it must pay the full commercial rent. If it pays less, HMRC taxes the shortfall as an unauthorised payment. If rent is left unpaid and not collected on normal terms, it can be treated as a loan to the company. A loan to the company must meet strict rules.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles; PTM123100: loans, general principles · Law: Finance Act 2004, s.162

Where the rent goes

The rent is paid to the SSAS, and the income from most pension scheme investments is not taxed. For your company, rent paid for premises it uses for its business is normally an allowable expense, under the wholly and exclusively rule.

Sources HMRC: Tax on investments for pension trustees; BIM46801: rent and rates

Energy rating

The SSAS trustees are the landlord. In England and Wales, the minimum energy rules normally stop a landlord letting a commercial building with an EPC rating below E. This does not apply if a valid exemption is registered. A commercial building normally needs an EPC when it is sold or let.

Sources GOV.UK: Energy Performance Certificates for your business premises; Non-domestic private rented property: minimum energy efficiency standard, landlord guidance · Law: SI 2015/962 (minimum energy efficiency standards)

Borrowing to buy

How much

A SSAS can borrow up to 50% of its net value just before the new borrowing, counting any borrowing it already has. In a money purchase SSAS, the test is worked out for each member's arrangement. HMRC lets it borrow for any purpose the trustees are satisfied will benefit the scheme.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, ss.182 to 185 · HMRC: PTM124000: borrowing

If it borrows too much

If new borrowing takes the scheme over the limit, the excess normally faces a 40% scheme sanction charge. Pension law's own limit on borrowing does not apply to schemes with fewer than 100 members.

Sources HMRC: PTM124000: borrowing · Law: SI 2005/3378, reg 7(1)

A worked example

A SSAS with one member holds £400,000 and has no borrowing. Just before it borrows, it can borrow up to half of that: £200,000. It buys the company's premises in England for £550,000, the price an independent valuer confirms is market value. The building is not new and the seller has not opted to tax, so there is no VAT on the price.

Stamp duty land tax is nothing on the first £150,000, 2% on the next £100,000 (£2,000) and 5% on the remaining £300,000 (£15,000). That makes £17,000.

Price of the premises£550,000
Stamp duty land tax (England)£17,000
Borrowed (the 50% limit)£200,000
Paid from the SSAS's own money£367,000
Left in the SSAS, before fees£33,000

The company then pays the SSAS a full market rent, set by a valuer. The SSAS uses the rent to repay its loan and to build up the pension, and pays no tax on the rent. The company can normally deduct the rent as a business expense. The SSAS will also pay legal, valuation and SSAS firm fees.

Sources Steve's analysis: The worked example · GOV.UK: Stamp Duty Land Tax: non-residential and mixed rates · HMRC: PTM124000: borrowing; Tax on investments for pension trustees; BIM46801: rent and rates

The steps

  1. Check the property is commercial, with no home in it unless an exception applies.
  2. Get the price checked by an independent valuer, so you can show it is market value.
  3. Check the VAT position, including whether the seller has opted to tax and whether the building is new.
  4. Arrange any borrowing within the 50% limit.
  5. The trustees buy the property through a solicitor, who usually sends the stamp duty return and pays the tax within 14 days.
  6. The trustees grant your company a lease at a full market rent.
  7. Collect the rent on time, keep the EPC up to date, and meet the minimum energy standard.

Common questions

Can my company use the building rent free?

No. Your company must pay the full commercial rent. If it pays less, HMRC taxes the shortfall as an unauthorised payment.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles

Can my SSAS buy property from me?

Yes, but only at market value. You may have Capital Gains Tax to pay on any gain, and the SSAS pays stamp duty land tax.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles · GOV.UK: Capital Gains Tax for business; Stamp Duty Land Tax: non-residential and mixed rates

Can my SSAS buy a holiday let or a buy-to-let flat?

Not without heavy tax charges. Homes are taxable property for a SSAS, wherever they are.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, Sch 29A, paras 7, 8, 10 and 14 · HMRC: PTM125300: direct holdings of taxable property

Can my SSAS build or convert property?

It can put up a commercial building to rent out. But building to sell at a profit may be trading, and trading profits do not get the pension tax exemptions. Once a building is ready to live in while the SSAS owns it, it counts as a home.

Sources HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles; BIM20205: badges of trade; PTM125200: residential property

What does buying a property cost?

As well as the price and stamp duty land tax, SSAS firms usually charge a fee for each purchase. For example, Highstone charges £750 and Mattioli Woods £1,190, both plus VAT. Some firms also charge a yearly fee for each property: Mattioli Woods charges £740 a year plus VAT. There are also legal and valuation costs.

Sources Provider evidence: SSAS provider fees compared, 2026 · GOV.UK: Stamp Duty Land Tax: non-residential and mixed rates
