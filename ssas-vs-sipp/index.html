SSAS or SIPP: what is the difference?

By Steve Hunt ACII TEP · Last checked 5 October 2026

The short answer: a SSAS is a company pension scheme that your company sets up. Its members are usually its trustees, so they control how the money is invested. A SIPP is a personal pension run by a SIPP firm: you choose your investments, but only from what the SIPP firm will accept. A SSAS that meets the small scheme conditions and HMRC's rules can lend money to its sponsoring company. A SIPP cannot do that for a company connected with its member. For a money purchase SSAS and a SIPP, the main tax rules on paying in and taking money out are broadly the same.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.150(5); Finance Act 2004, s.228 · MoneyHelper: SSAS pensions explained; SIPPs explained · FCA: Glossary: establishing, operating or winding up a personal pension scheme; Dear CEO letter: due diligence for SIPP investments, 30 October 2018 · HMRC: PTM123200: loans to sponsoring employers

The big, big difference

So many people confuse a SIPP with a SSAS. In this respect the two could not be more different.

With a SIPP, even though you think you have control because it is self-invested, it is still a personal pension. The true control sits with the SIPP firm and, where there is one, its trustee. They let you invest within certain parameters: you choose from the investments the SIPP firm will accept. So it is self-invested with a small s and a small i. That is the big, big difference.

With a SSAS, you are usually a trustee. The business owner is a trustee, the business owner shares the responsibility, and the business owner decides, with any fellow trustees, how the money is invested.

A SSAS is a small company pension scheme, but it is still a company pension scheme. The SSAS itself is regulated by The Pensions Regulator, not by the FCA in the way a SIPP is, and that in itself is a big thing.

The way I explain it is this. The Mr Miggins Ltd pension scheme is the same kind of scheme as the BP pension scheme: both are trust-based company pension schemes, just on completely different scales. The BP pension scheme's billions are invested under the control of its trustees, even though they can hand day-to-day investment decisions to experts. In the same way, you, as a trustee of the Mr Miggins Ltd pension scheme, decide with your fellow trustees how your company pension scheme's money is invested. You have control, as a trustee. It is Self-Invested with a capital S and a capital I.

What is the same

For a money purchase SSAS and a SIPP, many of the main pension tax rules are the same. A final salary (defined benefit) SSAS works differently in some areas.

  • The annual allowance: £60,000 a year across all your pensions, with the same carry forward rules.
  • Company tax relief: Your company's contributions to either can normally be deducted for corporation tax.
  • Taking money out: The same ages, the same tax-free cash rules and the same income tax rules.
  • Death benefits and inheritance tax: From 6 April 2027, most unused funds and pension death benefits from either can be included in the estate for inheritance tax, subject to the exclusions in the law.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.228; Finance Act 2004, s.196; Finance Act 2004, ss.165 and 279; Finance Act 2004, Sch 29; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A · HMRC: PTM121000: investments, essential principles

Moving a SIPP into a SSAS

The law allows money to move from one registered pension scheme to another, as a recognised transfer. Before the money goes, the SIPP firm must run the legal scam checks. Because a SSAS is an occupational scheme, you normally need to show a real job link with its employer. The evidence is usually a letter from the employer, a schedule of contributions, and three months of payslips or similar salary evidence. A red flag stops the transfer under these rules. An amber flag means you must first have a safeguarding guidance session with MoneyHelper.

In June 2026 the government proposed a new red flag for transfers into a SSAS where you cannot show a real job link. It is a proposal, not yet law.

Sources HMRC: PTM100010: transfers · Law: SI 2021/1237, regs 8 to 11 · TPR: Dealing with transfer requests · DWP: Protecting pension savers: options assessment, 9 June 2026

Moving a SSAS into a SIPP

This can also be a recognised transfer. Assets such as business property can move across as they are, if the SIPP firm accepts them, or they can be sold first and the cash moved. A loan from the SSAS to your company cannot move into a SIPP, because a SIPP lending to a company connected with you would be an unauthorised payment. If the SSAS carries on, the loan may stay in it, if the scheme rules and the transfer values allow. If the SSAS is being wound up, the loan must be repaid or dealt with first.

Sources HMRC: PTM100010: transfers; PTM123200: loans to sponsoring employers; PTM123300: loans to members and connected parties · Law: Finance Act 2004, s.161(5)

This is information only. It is not a recommendation to move a pension. Moving a pension can mean losing valuable benefits, and this site does not advise on transfers.

Things to compare

  • Control and responsibility: SSAS trustees decide how the money is invested, and are responsible for it. A SIPP firm runs a SIPP.
  • Loans to your company: only a SSAS can make them, under strict rules.
  • Fees: SSAS fees are flat. SIPP fees vary from firm to firm.
  • Decisions: in a SSAS with several members, the trustees make decisions together.
  • A company behind it: a SSAS needs a sponsoring employer. A SIPP does not.

Common questions

What is the main difference between a SSAS and a SIPP?

The main difference is who runs the pension. In a SSAS the members are usually the trustees, so they decide how the money is invested, and are responsible for it. In a SIPP, the SIPP firm runs the scheme, and you choose your investments from what it will accept.

Sources MoneyHelper: SSAS pensions explained · Law: Pensions Act 1995, s.34(1) · FCA: Dear CEO letter: due diligence for SIPP investments, 30 October 2018

Can a SIPP lend money to my company?

No. Only an occupational pension scheme, such as a SSAS, can lend to its sponsoring employer under HMRC's rules. A SIPP lending to a company connected with you would be an unauthorised payment.

Sources HMRC: PTM123200: loans to sponsoring employers; PTM123300: loans to members and connected parties

Can I move my SIPP into a SSAS?

The law allows it, as a recognised transfer, if the SSAS accepts it. The SIPP firm must first run the legal scam checks, including proof of a real job link with the SSAS's employer. Moving a pension can mean losing valuable benefits, and this site does not advise on transfers.

Sources HMRC: PTM100010: transfers · Law: SI 2021/1237, regs 8 to 11 · TPR: Dealing with transfer requests

Are SSASs and SIPPs taxed differently?

For a money purchase SSAS and a SIPP, the main pension tax rules are broadly the same: the standard annual allowance, the rules for taking money out, and the main death benefit rules. A final salary (defined benefit) SSAS works differently in some areas, including how its annual allowance is measured.

Sources Law: Finance Act 2004, s.228; Inheritance Tax Act 1984, s.150A
